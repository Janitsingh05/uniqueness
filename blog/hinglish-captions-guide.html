How to Caption Hinglish Videos Accurately

Why most auto-caption tools fall apart on Hindi-English mixed speech, and what actually gets it right.

Updated August 2026 · 6 min read

A huge amount of everyday Indian speech isn't purely Hindi or purely English — it's both, switching mid-sentence, sometimes mid-word. "Yaar this is actually kaafi accha" is completely normal spoken Hinglish, and it's exactly the kind of sentence that breaks caption tools built around picking a single language and sticking to it.

Why Hinglish is genuinely harder to transcribe

Most speech-to-text systems are trained to expect one language per utterance, and they make an internal decision — sometimes per sentence, sometimes per whole clip — about which language they're listening for. When speech code-switches, one of three things typically happens:

  • The English words get transcribed phonetically as if they were Hindi, producing nonsense text
  • The Hindi words get dropped or mangled because the model has locked onto "English mode"
  • The whole line gets transcribed in the wrong script entirely — Devanagari when you wanted Roman, or vice versa

None of these are edge cases for Indian creators — they're the default failure mode of tools that treat Hinglish as an afterthought rather than a real, common way people actually talk.

What actually helps

Transcription that expects code-switching

The fix isn't a bigger model — it's a transcription pipeline that doesn't assume monolingual input in the first place. That's a meaningfully different problem from "supports Hindi" and "supports English" as two separate settings; it means the system needs to handle a sentence changing language mid-stream without losing the plot.

Editable, not just automatic

Even good automatic transcription will occasionally misread a word, especially with heavy accents or fast, casual speech. What matters is whether fixing it is easy — a caption tool where you can edit the script directly and have the timing re-match automatically saves you from choosing between "perfect but manual" and "automatic but wrong."

Word-level timing that survives your edits

Once you fix a mistranscribed word, does the caption timing still line up? Tools that only time whole lines tend to fall apart here — see how word-level caption timing works for why per-word locking matters more than it sounds like it should.

A quick real-world test

If you want to check whether a caption tool actually handles Hinglish well, don't test it on a clean, single-language sentence — that's not the hard case. Test it on something that switches mid-thought, spoken at normal conversational speed, the way you'd actually talk in a Reel. That's the test that separates tools that support Hindi and English from tools that support Hinglish.
